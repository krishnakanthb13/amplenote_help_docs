# Naming your tag hierarchy: five recommendations

> [← Help Index](../00-index.md) · Category: [Organize Everything](./index.md) · [Source ↗](https://www.amplenote.com/help/five_recommendations_tag_hierarchy_naming)

## Overview

A "tag hierarchy" uses forward slashes to denote nested categories, like `personal/friends`. Unlike traditional directory systems, Amplenote's tag hierarchies allow notes to exist in multiple hierarchies simultaneously, enabling more flexible organization.

## Five recommendations for choosing tag names

### 1. Don't get fancy

Keep tag systems simple and sustainable. Complex productivity systems aren't built for long-term use.

**Suggestion:** "Use 10 or less tags at any level. This maxes you out at 100 second-level tags."

### 2. Prefer short paths with non-pluralized, non-abbreviated words

Use singular forms consistently (e.g., `homework/example` rather than `homework/examples`). Avoid abbreviations in second- and third-level tags to reduce accidental duplication.

**Suggestions:**

- Keep hierarchies three or fewer levels deep
- Use five or fewer total cumulative words
- Employ singular word forms for consistency
- Avoid abbreviations when practical

### 3. First-level tags are buckets

Top-level tags should serve as broad categories. Popular options include:

- `todo` – actively working tasks
- Project or hobby names – areas requiring multiple subtags
- `archive` – less-useful content
- `business` – work-related catch-all
- `canonical` – trusted primary sources
- `daily-jots` – morning notes
- `meetings` – discussion notes and takeaways
- `pdfs` – PDF-containing notes
- `people` – personal knowledge management
- `personal` – non-work content
- `projects` – work project organization
- `shared` – shared note tracking
- `travel` – travel-related content
- `weblinks` – bookmarked websites

**Avoid naming buckets after temporary interests.**

### 4. Second-level should serve a contextual purpose

Ensure second-level tags accumulate at least 5 notes yearly. Small note collections work better as linked notes rather than separate tags.

**Suggestions:**

- Don't maintain tags with fewer than 5 notes annually
- Use tags that inspire exploration and creativity
- Consider what unexplored content areas interest you

### 5. Third-level when you need to go deep

Third-level tags work best for task-focused hierarchies and specialized domains. Examples include `todo/personal/chore`, `dissertation/chapter-1/example`, and `uw/cse/143`.

**Suggestions:**

- Limit third-level tags to those accumulating 5+ annual notes
- Remember that notes can have multiple tags simultaneously

## Evolving your tag hierarchy

Amplenote supports easy tag restructuring: renaming, merging, and deleting hierarchies without removing associated notes. Review your tags quarterly, removing those with fewer than three notes after several months of use.
